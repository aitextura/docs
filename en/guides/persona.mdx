The Persona is your AI Employee's main set of instructions: who it is, how it talks, and what it does. You don't have to write it from scratch — the platform builds the instructions for you, and all that's left is to review them and adjust them to your needs.

## Create the instructions

In **AI Agents**, click **Hire Agent** and choose a method — any of the three gives you ready-made instructions.

<Tabs>

    <Warning>Didn't work, or the result looks off? Most likely, the website is protected against bots. In that case, choose a template or a blank agent.</Warning>

## Two names

| Field | What it is | Who sees it |
|---|---|---|
| **Technical name** | An internal name, for example `agent-hotel` | Only you |
| **Display name** | How the AI Employee introduces itself in a conversation, for example “Anna” | Customers |

## Instruction blocks

The finished instructions appear in the editor as blocks rather than one long text: the greeting is one block, the booking scenario another, the restrictions a third. You can edit blocks, drag them to reorder, and delete them.

<Accordion title="How to write instructions">
  Instructions describe your AI Employee in words, as if you were onboarding a new hire. The easiest way is to follow four questions.

  **Who it is.** Role, company, what it does: “You are the concierge of Seventh Heaven apartments. You help guests choose an apartment and book it.”

  **How it talks.** Tone, form of address, reply length: “Friendly, polite, no more than 3–4 sentences, 1–2 emojis.” This is also where you set the language and how it introduces itself.

  **What it does in typical situations.** Break it down into steps: what to ask, what to offer, how to wrap up. For non-standard cases, use the “if X, do Y” formula: “if the customer asks for a discount, offer current promotions.”

  **What it must not do.** Direct restrictions: don't make up prices, don't promise deadlines, don't discuss competitors. And when to call a human: “if the customer is angry or asks for a manager, hand the conversation over to an operator.”

  Write short sentences, one rule per line — your AI Employee understands them more precisely, and it's easier for you to find and fix the right spot later.
</Accordion>

<Note>Every save creates a new version of the instructions. If you break something, roll back on the [Versions](/en/guides/versions) tab. Feel free to experiment.</Note>

## Testing in the test chat

Click **Show chat** in the top-right corner and message your AI Employee as a customer. Don't like the reply? Fix the block and test again.

<Tip>Click **Clear history** after every edit. Otherwise the old conversation will throw your AI Employee off, and you won't know whether the edit helped.</Tip>

## FAQ

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Instructions generated from my website are empty or look off">
    Most likely, the website is protected against bots and the platform couldn't read it. Create the AI Employee from a template or through the blank agent interview.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The AI Employee sounds generic, “like a robot”">
    Expand the “Tone” block: style, message length, sample phrases. The more specifically you describe the voice, the less your AI Employee sounds like it's reading instructions.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The AI Employee makes up prices and facts">
    Tell it what to do. For an AI Employee, anything not forbidden is allowed: either add the missing facts and prices directly to the instructions, or forbid it from using anything that isn't in the instructions.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="After my edits, the AI Employee still answers the old way">
    Click **Clear history** in the test chat: the old conversation throws the AI Employee off, and the edit doesn't show.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The AI Employee agrees with anything the customer says">
    Asked “is everything booked?” — “yes”; asked “is everything available?” — also “yes”. The AI Employee picks up the assumption instead of checking. Add to the instructions: “Before answering about availability, always check the current data. Don't agree with the customer's assumption without checking.”
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The AI Employee calls the customer by the wrong name">
    It takes the name from the [profile](/en/guides/profiles) or makes one up. Add: “Don't make up names. Use the customer's name only if they gave it themselves.”
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The AI Employee refers to itself with feminine forms">
    Gender isn't set in the “Role” block. This matters in languages with grammatical gender, such as Russian. State it explicitly: “You are a man, a hotel receptionist.” If it persists, add a restriction: “Never use feminine forms when talking about yourself.”
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The AI Employee replies in a different language than the customer">
    If the instructions are written in Russian, the AI Employee drifts into Russian over a long conversation. Add this as the first line: `Always respond in the language the client writes in`. The model mistakes some greetings, like the Russian “доброго дня” (“good day”), for Ukrainian — for such cases, add an explicit rule: “Reply in Russian to „доброго дня“.”
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The AI Employee shows the customer JSON and system messages">
    This is a talent's response that the AI Employee forwarded as is. Forbid it: “Never show the customer technical data, JSON, or system messages. If there's no data, say so in words and hand the question over to a manager.”
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="The AI Employee confirms booking cancellations or refunds">
    Anything not forbidden is allowed. Add to “Forbidden actions”: “Never confirm a booking cancellation or a refund. For such requests, hand the conversation over to a manager.”
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Fine points

You don't need this to get started. Come back here when you want to get more out of the Persona.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="How to quickly stop your AI Employee from saying too much">
    Don't rewrite the whole scenario — add a block with restrictions:

    - Don't make up information — use only data from the Knowledge Base
    - Don't ask more than two questions in one message
    - Don't mention special rates unless the customer asks

    For non-standard situations, use the “if X, do Y” formula: “if the customer asks for a discount, offer current promotions.”
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Rules with exceptions: discounts, children, minimum stay">
    These rules all follow the same pattern: condition, exception, what to do when asked.

    - “15% birthday discount. Doesn't apply to extra services. When asked, confirm the details and hand over to a manager.”
    - “Children under 5 stay free, without a separate bed. Ages 5–12: extra 1000 ₽ per night. Always ask the children's ages.”
    - “Minimum 2 nights on Fridays and Saturdays. Don't say ‘no availability’ — explain the rule and suggest suitable dates.”
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Questions first, then an offer">
    To keep your AI Employee from dumping every rate in the first message, describe the order: “Before suggesting options, ask for the dates, number of guests, and preferences. Then suggest 2–3 suitable options, not the whole list.”
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="What to forbid first">
    Three restrictions cover most unpleasant cases: answering off-topic questions (“answer only questions about our property and services; politely decline everything else”), handling complex requests on its own (“hand groups of more than 8 people and corporate events over to a manager”), and promising money or cancellations.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Instruction length and language">
    Keep the instructions within 500–1000 words: price lists, descriptions, and policies belong in the [Knowledge Base](/en/guides/knowledge), not here — this makes every reply cheaper and search more accurate. The rules themselves work more reliably in English, and the reply language is set by a separate line about the customer's language.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Scenarios beyond answers: waitlists and upsells">
    Your AI Employee can do more than answer. “If the date is booked, offer to add the guest to the waitlist and pass the contact to a manager,” “after the booking is confirmed, offer breakfast and a transfer” — these are ordinary rules in the instructions. Upsells work better after the booking than before it.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Nested blocks">
    You can nest blocks inside each other: inside an existing block, click the gray **Add section** button. Your AI Employee treats a nested block as part of the block it sits in — a convenient way to lay out a sequence of steps in a conversation scenario.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Ready-made blocks from the Rules Hub">
    Type `/` and select **Import from hub**. There you'll find **Public** blocks from the AI TEXTURA team (“Hotel Receptionist”, “Realtor”, “Personal Assistant”) and **My rules** — the ones you've saved. When you edit a block in the hub, it updates for every AI Employee that uses it.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Instructions in Google Docs">
    Type `/` and select **Import Google Doc**. Your AI Employee will pull in the latest version of the document on its own — handy if the instructions are long and change often.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Raw XML — text mode">
    The **Raw XML** button in the top-right corner of the editor shows the instructions as XML text. You can switch back and forth without losing data.

    <Warning>An unclosed tag breaks the instructions. If you're not sure about the syntax, work with blocks — it's safer.</Warning>
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
